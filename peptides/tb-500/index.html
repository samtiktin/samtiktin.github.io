TB-500: research overview and suppliers

A research peptide sold under the name of its parent protein, thymosin beta-4. What a vendor means by TB-500 is not standardized.

Also known as: Thymosin beta-4 fragment. For laboratory research use only.

What it is

  • Parent protein: Thymosin beta-4, 43 amino acids (about 4,963 Da)
  • TB-500 usage: Varies by vendor; often a short fragment
  • Typical form: Lyophilized powder

Thymosin beta-4 is a 43-amino-acid protein studied in cell biology for its role in actin regulation. Vendors market TB-500 as a synthetic version, but the term is used loosely: some sell the full-length protein, others a short active-region fragment, and the two have very different molecular weights.

That ambiguity is the main thing to resolve before buying. A COA that shows a mass should let you tell which one you have.

Batch-record checklist

Before relying on any vial, check the documentation. For TB-500 specifically:

  • Ask which sequence the vendor means by TB-500 and check the COA mass against it.
  • A full-length thymosin beta-4 should read near 4,963 Da; a short fragment will be far lower.
  • Compare vial size and listed peptide content when comparing prices across vendors.

For every compound:

  • A COA dated and numbered for the batch printed on your vial, not a generic or reused file.
  • HPLC purity with the trace shown, and mass-spec confirming identity.
  • The testing lab's name and a way to verify the report independently.
  • Fill amount, counter-ion and storage guidance on the label or listing.
