What it is

GLOW at a glance

GLOW is not a single peptide. It is a supplier-coined name for a pre-mixed blend that combines three separately studied compounds in one vial: GHK-Cu, BPC-157, and TB-500.

The most common published ratio is 50mg GHK-Cu, 10mg BPC-157, and 10mg TB-500 for a 70mg total blend, though suppliers can vary the exact milligram split, so the label on any given listing should always be checked directly.

Research context

Where current research places it

Skin, collagen, and wound-healing research (from GHK-Cu) alongside tissue-repair and tendon/ligament research (from BPC-157 and TB-500).

Preclinical for all three ingredients individually; the combined blend itself has no dedicated trial record.

What research has looked at

What each ingredient contributes

Reading a blend as its parts, not as one compound

GHK-Cu is a copper-binding tripeptide studied for roles in collagen synthesis, extracellular matrix remodeling, and antioxidant activity, which is why it anchors the "glow" positioning around skin-related research.

BPC-157 and TB-500 (thymosin beta-4) are both studied in tissue-repair and wound-healing models, including tendon, ligament, and gastrointestinal research for BPC-157, and angiogenesis and actin-binding research for TB-500.

Because the blend combines three independently studied peptides rather than testing the mixture itself, a reader should evaluate the documentation for each ingredient separately rather than treating "GLOW" as a single research-backed entity.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers to the main questions that come up on this page.

What is GLOW?

GLOW is a supplier-branded name for a peptide blend that combines GHK-Cu, BPC-157, and TB-500 in one vial, most commonly formulated as 50mg GHK-Cu, 10mg BPC-157, and 10mg TB-500 (a 70mg total blend). It is not itself a single studied compound; it is a mixture of three separately studied peptides.

What research areas is it linked to?

Each ingredient carries its own research base: GHK-Cu is linked to skin, collagen, and wound-healing research; BPC-157 and TB-500 are linked to tissue-repair and tendon/ligament research. There is little to no published literature testing the specific 50/10/10 combination itself.

Is GLOW the same at every supplier?

Not necessarily. "GLOW" is a marketing name rather than a regulated term, so the exact milligram ratio of GHK-Cu, BPC-157, and TB-500 can differ between suppliers. Always check the specific label.

Has the GLOW combination itself been studied?

Not as a combined formula. The published research covers each ingredient individually rather than the specific pre-mixed blend sold under this name.

What should stand out when comparing supplier pages?

Look for a supplier page that lists the exact milligram breakdown of each ingredient, provides a batch-specific COA covering the finished blend (not just the individual raw peptides), and avoids treating the combination as if it had its own dedicated clinical evidence.
