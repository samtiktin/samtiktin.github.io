BPC-157: research overview and suppliers

A 15-amino-acid synthetic peptide derived from a sequence found in human gastric juice protein, studied almost entirely in rodent models.

Also known as: Body Protection Compound-157, pentadecapeptide BPC 157. For laboratory research use only.

What it is

  • Length: 15 amino acids
  • Sequence: GEPPPGKPADDAGLV
  • Approx. mass: about 1,419 Da
  • Typical form: Lyophilized powder

BPC-157 is a synthetic fragment modeled on part of a protein found in gastric juice. Nearly all published work comes from a small number of laboratories using rodent models, covering tissue-repair, gastrointestinal and vascular endpoints. Large, controlled human trials have not been published, and the compound is not an approved drug.

For a buyer this matters in one practical way: you cannot lean on clinical-grade supply chains. Identity and purity depend entirely on the vendor's own testing, so the batch record is the product.

Batch-record checklist

Before relying on any vial, check the documentation. For BPC-157 specifically:

  • Mass-spec identity should match roughly 1,419 Da for the free peptide.
  • Confirm the COA names BPC-157 (the 15-mer), not a salt form or a longer variant, and states the net peptide content if given.
  • Some vendors sell an arginate or acetate form; the label and COA should say which.

For every compound:

  • A COA dated and numbered for the batch printed on your vial, not a generic or reused file.
  • HPLC purity with the trace shown, and mass-spec confirming identity.
  • The testing lab's name and a way to verify the report independently.
  • Fill amount, counter-ion and storage guidance on the label or listing.
