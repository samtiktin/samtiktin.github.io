MOTS-c: research overview and suppliers

A 16-amino-acid peptide encoded in mitochondrial DNA, studied in metabolism and aging research in cell and animal models.

Also known as: Mitochondrial open reading frame of the 12S rRNA-c. For laboratory research use only.

What it is

  • Length: 16 amino acids
  • Origin: Encoded in mitochondrial DNA (12S rRNA)
  • Approx. mass: about 2,174 Da
  • Typical form: Lyophilized powder

MOTS-c belongs to a group of mitochondrial-derived peptides described in the 2010s. It has drawn interest in metabolic and exercise-physiology research, mostly in rodent and cell studies, with limited human data.

It is a mid-sized peptide with a methionine-rich sequence, which makes it somewhat sensitive to oxidation. Handling and storage notes on the COA and label are worth reading.

Batch-record checklist

Before relying on any vial, check the documentation. For MOTS-c specifically:

  • COA mass should be near 2,174 Da.
  • Methionine residues oxidize; an oxidized peak in HPLC or MS is a quality flag.
  • Look for storage guidance and a date of manufacture.

For every compound:

  • A COA dated and numbered for the batch printed on your vial, not a generic or reused file.
  • HPLC purity with the trace shown, and mass-spec confirming identity.
  • The testing lab's name and a way to verify the report independently.
  • Fill amount, counter-ion and storage guidance on the label or listing.
