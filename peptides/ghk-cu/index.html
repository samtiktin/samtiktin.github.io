GHK-Cu: research overview and suppliers

A naturally occurring tripeptide that binds copper, widely used in cosmetic ingredient research and studied in cell and tissue models.

Also known as: Copper tripeptide-1, glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine copper. For laboratory research use only.

What it is

  • Length: 3 amino acids (Gly-His-Lys)
  • Form: Copper(II) complex
  • Approx. mass: about 404 Da as the complex
  • Appearance: Blue powder

GHK was first isolated from human plasma in the 1970s and binds copper(II) with high affinity. It appears in a large body of in-vitro and cosmetic-science literature, and copper-peptide complexes are common cosmetic ingredients.

Because the active species is a metal complex, quality questions differ from a plain peptide: copper content and color matter, and an off-color powder can indicate a problem.

Batch-record checklist

Before relying on any vial, check the documentation. For GHK-Cu specifically:

  • The powder should be a distinct blue; white or gray suggests missing copper.
  • Look for a copper assay or ICP result in addition to HPLC purity.
  • Check whether the vial is the copper complex or free GHK; they are different products.

For every compound:

  • A COA dated and numbered for the batch printed on your vial, not a generic or reused file.
  • HPLC purity with the trace shown, and mass-spec confirming identity.
  • The testing lab's name and a way to verify the report independently.
  • Fill amount, counter-ion and storage guidance on the label or listing.
