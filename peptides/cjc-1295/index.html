CJC-1295: research overview and suppliers

A GHRH analog sold in two chemically different forms under similar names. Confirming which one you are buying is the main task.

Also known as: With DAC; without DAC is Modified GRF (1-29). For laboratory research use only.

What it is

  • Two forms: With DAC and without DAC
  • No-DAC form: Modified GRF (1-29), 29 amino acids
  • Class: Growth hormone-releasing hormone analog
  • Typical form: Lyophilized powder

CJC-1295 is a growth hormone-releasing hormone analog. The name covers two products: a version with a drug affinity complex (DAC) that extends half-life, and a version without it, usually called Modified GRF (1-29) or CJC-1295 no DAC.

Vendors use the names inconsistently, and some blends say only CJC-1295. The COA mass is the quickest way to tell the two forms apart.

Batch-record checklist

Before relying on any vial, check the documentation. For CJC-1295 specifically:

  • Check the product title for DAC or no DAC and match it to the COA mass.
  • The no-DAC form is the shorter peptide; the DAC form carries an added linker and is heavier.
  • For blends, confirm both components and their amounts are listed.

For every compound:

  • A COA dated and numbered for the batch printed on your vial, not a generic or reused file.
  • HPLC purity with the trace shown, and mass-spec confirming identity.
  • The testing lab's name and a way to verify the report independently.
  • Fill amount, counter-ion and storage guidance on the label or listing.
