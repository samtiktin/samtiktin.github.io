Portman Peptides review

By the PeptideSuppliers.org editorial team · How we review

Portman Peptides is a UK, enquiry-led supplier of BPC-157, TB-500, and GHK-Cu for non-human laboratory research. There is no live checkout: a buyer sends a specification, receives a written quote, and pays by bank transfer against an invoice. This is a factual directory profile. It carries no star rating and no testing or verification badge, and it does not state a price or stock position.

At a glance

What Portman is

A UK-focused supplier with a deliberately narrow, enquiry-first catalogue. Its site is built around buying guides and checklists that tell a laboratory what to ask for before buying, rather than around a shopping cart.

  • Market: UK research enquiries (England, Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland)
  • Materials: BPC-157, TB-500, and GHK-Cu
  • Ordering: written quote, then invoice paid by bank transfer
  • Use: genuine non-human laboratory research only; not for human or veterinary use
Buyer fit

Who this may suit

UK laboratory buyers who are comfortable requesting a specification, lot records, and a full delivered quote by email, and who want to review those before paying. It is not suited to buyers who want to compare prices at a glance or check out online.

Catalogue and enquiries

Three materials, each handled by enquiry.

Catalogue

BPC-157, TB-500, and GHK-Cu

Portman lists three materials. For each, the buyer contacts Portman with the required material specification, pack quantity, and the documents needed. Availability, the offered lot, and commercial terms are confirmed directly by Portman. Material pages: BPC-157, TB-500, and GHK-Cu.

How an enquiry works

Specification first

Per its How to order page, the buyer states the material, exact specification, pack quantity, number of packs, delivery country, timing, and required documents. An enquiry does not reserve stock or create an order. Portman replies by email, and says each material and its offered lot must be assessed separately.

Prices and stock

Not stated here

The site shows an indicative figure for one pack size and no figure for the other two materials, and says prices, stock, and dispatch dates are confirmed by quotation. We do not reproduce a price or make any stock claim; ask Portman for a written offer.

Documents and testing

What the site says, and what it does not publish.

What is published

Buying guidance, not lot reports

We did not find batch reports, COAs, or a lot-record index on the pages we reviewed. What the site publishes is guidance: seven buying guides (BPC-157, TB-500, and GHK-Cu checklists, how to read a batch report, purity versus content, comparing delivered prices, and repeat procurement) and a quote-comparison calculator. They are generally sensible and consistent with how we assess suppliers.

What Portman says

Original records for the offered lot

Portman tells buyers to request the original records for the exact lot being offered and to check identity, batch identifier, issuing laboratory, methods, and scope. It states that current offered-lot documents must be confirmed as part of a written offer and that packaging imagery is not evidence of testing. We have not seen any such records.

Imagery

Packaging concepts

Product photography is labeled as packaging concepts with illustrative label quantities. The pack sizes and labels shown are not specifications.

How to use this

A practical check before ordering

  • Send the exact material, form, pack size, and quantity in writing
  • Ask for the original analytical report for the lot that will be supplied, and confirm which lot that is
  • Check the report names the lab, methods, and date, and see how the tested sample relates to the offered lot
  • Get delivered price, tax, dispatch, and seller details in the written offer before paying

Ordering and payment

Quote, invoice, bank transfer.

Process

Five steps from enquiry to dispatch

Portman describes: send the requirement; review the quote and evidence; accept the offer and check the invoice; pay by bank transfer to the verified details; then payment confirmation and dispatch under the agreed terms. It says a screenshot of a transfer is not payment confirmation.

Payment

Bank transfer only

The site says it does not take card payments. Bank transfers are hard to reverse, so check the invoice carefully: the seller, order, payee, amount, and reference. Portman advises that if bank details ever change you should confirm the change through the existing contact thread before paying; take that advice.

Delivery

Terms in the written offer

Enquiries cover England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland. Dispatch time, delivery charge, tax, and seller details belong in the written offer. We found no standard delivery times, returns policy, or refund terms on the pages we reviewed, so ask for them in the quote.

Company and contact

Who it is and how to reach it.

Business details

A trading name

The site says “Portman Peptides is the trading name of Portman Smith.” We did not find a registered company number, a physical address, or a phone number on the pages we reviewed. Its pages say seller contact and address details must be supplied before a payable offer, so ask for them in writing.

Contact

Email

Portman’s stated contact is info@portmanpeptides.com. The site also has an enquiry form.

Research framing

Non-human laboratory research only

Every page we reviewed states the materials are for genuine non-human laboratory research only, not for human or veterinary use, and that Portman gives no dosing or administration advice. Nothing on this page is usage guidance.

No published documents

Evidence is on request

There is nothing public to check. Whether a buyer gets lot-specific records depends on the quote process.

Quote-only buying

Hard to compare

Without public prices on most materials, buyers must request quotes to compare Portman with other suppliers, and should compare delivered cost, not the product line.

Bank transfer only

Little recourse after payment

With no card option, there is no chargeback route. Verify the invoice and the bank details before sending money.

Limited company detail

Trading name only

We found no registered company number, address, or phone number. Ask before paying.

Cart buttons

“Add to cart” on a quote-only site

Pages show Add to cart buttons and a cart, but the site says no payment is taken online and terms are confirmed by quotation. Treat them as enquiry starters.

Common questions

Send an enquiry with the material, specification, pack size, quantity, and documents you need. Portman replies with a written quote; you pay the invoice by bank transfer. There is no online checkout.

Summary

An enquiry-first supplier with nothing public to verify yet

Portman’s approach is unusually procedural: its guides coach buyers to ask for lot records, delivered prices, and seller details, which is the right instinct. But the site publishes no lot reports and few prices, so the buying decision rests on what comes back in a quote. This profile gives no rating or badge and does not verify the material. If Portman later publishes lot reports, or a buyer or we complete an enquiry, we will update this page.

Bottom line

Quick take

Reasonable to approach if you are a UK lab buyer who will ask for the lot report first. Compare the full delivered quote, and verify the invoice before paying by bank transfer.
