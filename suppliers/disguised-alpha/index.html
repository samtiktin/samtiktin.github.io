Supplier review

Disguised Alpha review

By the PeptideSuppliers.org editorial team · How we review

Disguised Alpha is an Irvine, California supplier with one of the largest public COA libraries we have seen in this directory: 180 products and 407 report records from independent laboratories, with QR and access-code links back to the lab. It also runs a program that reimburses buyers for independent re-testing. Its weaker side is consistency: testing, purity, and payment claims differ from page to page, and payment is mostly bank, Zelle, and crypto.

A large, searchable COA library and a re-test program

Testing and COAs in detail

What the library shows and where the claims do not line up.

The library

180 products, 407 reports, 3 labs

The COA portal says it has 180 products with reports, 407 published report records, and three laboratories represented, ILS Laboratories, Chromate, and Freedom Diagnostics in the entries we saw. Entries are grouped by product, with links to each batch and report and the lab and test date. Both current and historical reports are included, so check the date and which batch is currently shipping.

A sample report

ILS Laboratories, lot RTM01

The portal walks through an example ILS certificate for a retatrutide-class product, lot RTM01, tested July 26, 2026, and issued August 14, 2026. It lists a COA number, ISO/IEC 17025 as the standard, an overall PASS, and six checks: identity, purity, sterility, endotoxins, heavy metals, and a fentanyl screen. Purity on the example is 99.53% against a specification of at least 95%. A QR code and access code open the certificate on the lab’s portal.

How to read it

Batch number matters

The portal tells buyers to match product, labeled strength, and batch number, and warns that a laboratory report number is not always the production batch number. We agree with that framing, and it is why we suggest checking the vial’s batch against the report.

Claims that differ

Panels, purity, and testing scope

The home and About pages describe “8-panel batch testing” and a 99%+ purity floor, with anything below it “destroyed, not shipped.” The COA portal says up to 10 tests per batch “as applicable” and shows an example with six checks and a 95% specification. The About page lists sterility screening “mostly by PCR” and endotoxin screening “typically” under 0.5 EU/mg. The FAQ itself says not to assume every report includes every assay and that a general website statement is not a substitute for the actual report. We agree, so use the batch report.

How to use this

A practical check before ordering

  • Find your product and strength in the COA portal and note the batch and report number
  • Check the lab, test date, and which tests the report actually includes
  • Open the lab’s own verification using the QR code or access code
  • When the vial arrives, match the batch number on the label to the report

Verify Your Product program

Independent re-testing, with reimbursement.

How it works

Register, test, submit

Buyers register a test before sending a sample, use one of five approved laboratories (Janoshik, ILS, Chromate, Freedom, or Vanguard), pay the lab directly, and submit the final report and paid receipt. Disguised Alpha reimburses 100% of the eligible test cost as store credit once the documents are verified, and may confirm them with the lab.

The guarantee

2x order guarantee

If an approved lab reports a qualifying material issue that is verified against the order, the supplier offers a full refund of that order plus store credit equal to the refunded value. Pending, cancelled, or incomplete tests, and altered or unverifiable submissions, are not eligible.

The limits

Peptide vials only, store credit

The program covers lyophilized research peptides: 75 eligible products are listed. Capsules, aminos and oils, sprays, topicals, and solutions are outside it. Reimbursement is store credit, not cash, and the supplier says it does not control the labs or their results. It is still a notable way to back testing claims.

Discounts

Several overlapping offers

The site advertises 10% sitewide plus 10% with an affiliate code during a Halloween sale, a WELCOME10 code, payment-method discounts of 5–7%, a military and first responder discount, a referral program, a free gift over $300, and free FedEx 2-Day over $250. Eligible combinations are decided in the cart, so check the final total.

Shipping, payment and returns

Dispatch, accepted payments, and what happens if something goes wrong.

Shipping

Same-day dispatch from Irvine

Orders placed by 2:30 PM Pacific on weekdays, or 12:00 PM Pacific on Saturday, are dispatched the same day, subject to payment confirmation, review, and stock. FedEx 2-Day is free over $250, and PO Box addresses are routed through USPS. The site lists Canada and says it is shipping to Germany, France, and the UK. International buyers pay duties and taxes, and the supplier says it is not responsible for seized packages or customs delays.

Payment methods

Mostly bank, Zelle, Chime, and crypto

The Payment Methods page lists Bitcoin (7% off), Zelle, Chime, and bank payment through Plaid or Link Money (5% off each). The footer shows Zelle, Chime, and crypto. The shipping and returns page, in contrast, lists Cash App, Visa and Amex with no processing fee, and Mastercard, Discover, and Klarna as coming soon, and says no card transactions are allowed for international shipments. We could not confirm which cards work today. Zelle, bank transfer, and crypto are hard to reverse, and About describes a chargeback and mail-fraud policy that buyers sign at checkout.

Returns and damage

Final sale once shipped

No returns on opened or used products, and all sales are final once shipped. Report a damaged or incorrect item within 48 hours of delivery with photos. Orders cannot be changed once in fulfillment. Optional Route shipping protection may be offered at checkout.

Company and contact

Who is behind it and how to reach them.

Business details

Irvine, CA; operating entity unclear

Pages say the supplier is Irvine-based and fulfills from a single California facility. The footer says the site is “Powered by Sterling Path LLC,” and we did not find the legal operating entity, an owner, or a registered address on the pages we reviewed. Ask for these in writing if they matter to you.

Contact

Email, form, and phone hours

The business contact is support@disguisedalpha.com. The site also has a contact form, order tracking, and lists phone support from 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM Pacific. The FAQ says to include your order number and not to email card numbers or passwords.

Access

21+ and an account prompt

The site requires a 21+ confirmation and a research-use acknowledgment, and its pages show a “create your free account” prompt to browse. We could read the content without an account, but expect to register to buy. Nothing on this page is dosing or usage guidance.

Inconsistent testing claims

8 panels, 6 checks, 10 tests

The marketing language, the example report, and the portal describe different test panels and different purity floors. Rely on the batch report.

Payment methods differ

Cards listed on some pages only

Accepted payments vary by page. Confirm at checkout, and assume the main options are hard to reverse.

Chargeback policy

Signed at checkout

The site describes a chargeback and mail-fraud policy that buyers acknowledge at checkout. Read it before you pay.

Store credit remedies

Not cash

The re-test reimbursement and the guarantee extra are in store credit. A verified material issue still gets a full order refund.

Entity not stated

No legal name or address

We could not find the operating company or registered address on the pages we reviewed.

Editorial take

Deep documentation, uneven consistency

Disguised Alpha has gone further than most suppliers on documentation: a large library, lab verification links, a clear guide to reading a report, and money behind re-testing. What holds it back is that its own pages do not agree on how products are tested or how you can pay, and the main payment methods are hard to reverse. We are rating documentation and buyer risk, not product quality, because we have not tested product.

Bottom line

Quick verdict

Worth considering if you check the exact batch with the lab first, confirm payment options at checkout, and read the chargeback and returns terms before paying.
