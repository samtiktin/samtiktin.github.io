Supplier review

Ageless Peptides review

By the PeptideSuppliers.org editorial team · How we review

Ageless Peptides (also branded Ageless Protocol, with a Sheridan, Wyoming mailing address) is a US research-peptide storefront with a 29-product catalog of lyophilized vials, nasal sprays, and sublingual strips. It stands out for card checkout, simple bundle pricing, and fast stated handling. Documentation is the weak point: of six product pages we opened, one carried a lab report, and that report was issued to a different company.

How this review was made

Based on the supplier’s submission and its public pages — not on an order

The supplier sent us a summary of its catalog, pricing, and shipping and asked to be listed in research-use-only terms. We then checked those claims against ageless-protocol.com on October 13, 2026: the home page, the Shop pages, six product pages (BPC-157, the BPC-157/TB-500 blend, GHK-Cu, the GLOW blend, Tesamorelin, and the Selank + Semax spray), Terms, the Shipping & Refund Policy, Contact, the affiliate page, and the Longevity guide. We opened the one lab report we found. We have not placed an order, received product, or confirmed any report with a laboratory. The supplier runs an affiliate program for creators; the links on this page are plain links with no tracking code. See our disclosure for how listings are handled.

Why it stands out

Card checkout and simple bundle math

Most smaller peptide suppliers in this directory take only crypto or peer-to-peer apps. Ageless Peptides lists Visa, Mastercard, and American Express, and product pages also mention Apple Pay. Pricing is public, bundle discounts are laid out on each product page, and the Buy 2, Get 1 Free trios are a better deal than the volume tiers.

  • Public prices and per-product specifications, with no account needed

Testing and COAs in detail

What we found, what the report says, and what is missing.

What we found

One report across six product pages

The home page, Terms, and Shipping pages do not mention testing, purity, or a COA. Product pages for BPC-157, the BPC-157/TB-500 blend, the GLOW blend, Tesamorelin, and the Selank + Semax spray list a compound, content per vial, form, and intended use, but no purity figure and no lab report. The GHK-Cu 50mg page is the exception: it has a “View third-party lab report (COA)” panel with a summary table and a PDF link. We did not check all 29 product pages, so others may carry reports, but we could not find a COA index or lab-report page.

The one report

GHK-Cu 50mg, Vanguard Laboratory

The page summary reads: Vanguard Laboratory (citing A2LA Certificate #6377.01.01), HPLC purity above 99.8%, measured content 55.50 mg, endotoxin (LAL) pass, lot G26C922, reported 3/2/2026. We opened the PDF and those figures match. Purity is by HPLC-UV/VIS, and the endotoxin result is stated as under 5.00 EU/mg against a 5 EU/kg threshold.

The catch

The report is not addressed to Ageless

The PDF lists “New England Biologics” as the party the report was issued to, not Ageless Peptides. It is dated March 2, 2026, before the Ageless Peptides site was first published in May 2026, and the page calls it a “representative lot.” The lab also states in the report that it did not observe or take part in sample selection and cannot confirm the sample’s authenticity or whether it represents a given lot or batch. So the report shows that a GHK-Cu sample had that profile; it does not show what is in a vial shipped by Ageless. We cannot tell from public pages how New England Biologics relates to Ageless Peptides.

Worth noting

Measured content above the label

The report lists 55.50 mg for a 50 mg product, about 11% above the label. Purity and content are separate measurements and content results carry method variability, so this is not necessarily a problem, but it is the kind of number a buyer should ask about, and it comes from a single sample.

Blends

No report for pre-mixed vials

The GLOW (GHK-Cu/BPC-157/TB-500), KLOW, and BPC-157/TB-500 blends are sold as fixed-ratio single vials. A fixed ratio is only useful if it holds, and the way to show that is a report for that product. We did not see one. The GLOW blend at $79.99 is also about half the price of its three components bought separately ($159.97 at the single-vial prices listed), so asking for a blend-specific report is reasonable.

How to use this

A practical check before ordering

  • Ask support for the lab report for the exact product and lot before you order, and note the lot number
  • Check that the report names Ageless Peptides, or ask how the named company is connected
  • Confirm the report with the laboratory using the contact details printed on it
  • When the order arrives, match the lot on the vial to the report

Bundles and discounts

How the three discount types work, and which is best.

Volume tiers

10% off 3+, 15% off 6+

On each product page, three units of the same item get 10% off and six or more get 15% off. For BPC-157 10mg that is $44.99 each for three ($134.97) and $42.49 each for six ($254.95). The math on the pages checks out.

Trios

Buy 2, Get 1 Free

Trios are priced as two vials with the third free, which is one-third off: the GLOW Trio is $159.98 for three vials against $239.97 at single prices. That beats both the 10% and the 15% tiers, so where a trio exists it is the cheaper way to buy three. Trios are offered for GLOW, KLOW, Tesamorelin, 5-Amino-1MQ, and BPC-157.

Shipping threshold

Free over $75

Orders of $75 or more ship free. Below that, a product FAQ states shipping is $9.95, a fee that does not appear on the Shipping & Refund Policy, so confirm it at checkout. We did not find a discount code for peptides; the AGELESS10 code on the site is for the sister apparel brand.

Shipping, payment and refunds

Handling time, coverage, payment methods, and the returns policy.

Shipping

All 50 US states, shipped within 24 hours

The Shipping & Refund Policy (last updated September 25, 2026) says orders placed on a business day ship within 24 hours, and weekend or holiday orders ship the next business day. It ships to all 50 states via “standard carriers,” with tracking “where available.” Packaging is described as discreet. Some product pages say “Same-Day Shipping, orders before 2PM” instead of the 24-hour wording, so the exact cut-off is unclear. We found no international shipping; the site refers only to US delivery.

Payment methods

Visa, Mastercard, and American Express

The footer lists Visa, Mastercard, and Amex, and product pages say “card, Apple Pay & more.” We could not see the full checkout without placing an order. Card payments give buyers dispute and chargeback options that crypto, Zelle, and similar methods do not, which is a real point in the supplier’s favor.

Refunds and damage

Narrow, with a 7-day window

Returns are not accepted once a product has shipped, except for damage, defect, or a fulfillment error. Report those within 7 days of delivery with the order number and photos; resolution is a replacement or refund at the supplier’s discretion, and approved refunds go to the original payment method in 5–10 business days. Orders cannot be cancelled or changed once they enter fulfillment. Carrier delays are not guaranteed against.

Company and site details

Who is behind it, how to reach them, and a few things we noticed.

Business details

A mailing address and no named entity

Every page lists “Ageless Peptides · 30 N Gould St Ste N, Sheridan, WY 82801.” The Terms call it a “mailing address,” are governed by Wyoming law, and send disputes to courts in Sheridan. We did not find a legal entity name (such as an LLC), an owner, or a statement of where orders are packed and shipped from. The site goes by both Ageless Peptides and Ageless Protocol, and the domain is ageless-protocol.com.

Contact

Form and support email

The Contact page offers a form and a support email, support@ageless-protocol.com, with a stated response time of 24–48 business hours. When we loaded it, a developer notice about an inactive CAPTCHA plugin was visible on the form. That is cosmetic, but send a test message before you rely on the form.

Cart links

Check the cart on bundle buttons

On the home page, the Add to cart buttons for the 5-Amino-1MQ Trio and BPC-157 Trio use the same product ID as the GLOW Trio (337), while the Shop page uses different IDs for them (393 and 394). We did not place an order, so we cannot say what lands in the cart, but if you use the home-page buttons, check the cart contents before paying.

Other content

Longevity guide and apparel

The site includes a Longevity guide with general lifestyle articles on sleep, training, nutrition, and daily habits, plus links to a sister apparel brand, Gains Protocol. The guide does not discuss the compounds sold. It shows content effort for a new site, but it is not evidence about product quality.

Newness

Very short public track record

Pages date from May 2026, product pages show “Reviews (0)” on every page we opened, and we found no independent customer-review history. That is normal for a young shop, but it means there is little outside feedback to lean on.

Research framing

Consistent research-use-only language

Product descriptions and footers say the products are for laboratory research only and not for human or animal use, and a confirmation gate asks visitors to affirm they are qualified researchers. Terms say in vitro only, while some product pages say in vitro and animal-model research; that small inconsistency should be cleaned up.

Documentation gap

One report, issued to someone else

Only the GHK-Cu page has a lab report, and it is addressed to New England Biologics, predates the site, and carries the lab’s disclaimer that it cannot confirm the sample represents a lot. Treat the catalog as largely undocumented until you see a report for the product you want.

Blends are unverified

No visible report for fixed-ratio vials

The GLOW, KLOW, and BPC-157/TB-500 blends depend on the stated ratio being accurate. Without a report, that is a claim and not something you can check.

Unclear company details

No named owner or entity

A mailing address and a support email are the only identifiers. Order problems would be resolved at the supplier’s discretion under a narrow refund policy.

Inconsistent details

Shipping wording and cart links

The 24-hour versus same-day wording, the shipping fee missing from the policy, and the home-page cart IDs are small issues, but they show a site that is still being tidied.

Common questions

Quick answers about this supplier.

Does Ageless Peptides publish COAs?

We found one: a GHK-Cu 50mg report from Vanguard Laboratory on that product page. We did not find reports on the five other product pages we opened, and there is no COA index. The report is addressed to a different company, so ask the supplier for a report covering your product and lot.

Can I pay by card?

Yes. The site lists Visa, Mastercard, and American Express, and mentions Apple Pay. We did not complete a checkout.

Does it ship internationally?

We found no international shipping. The Shipping & Refund Policy covers all 50 US states only.

Is there free shipping?

Yes, on orders of $75 or more. A product FAQ states $9.95 shipping below that threshold.

Which bundle is the best value?

The Buy 2, Get 1 Free trios, which work out to one-third off, beat the 10% (3+) and 15% (6+) volume tiers.

Did you order from them?

No. This review is based on the supplier’s submission and public website, checked on October 13, 2026. If we order, we will say so on this page.

Is this for human use?

No. Everything is sold for laboratory research only, and nothing on this page is dosing or usage guidance.

Editorial take

Convenient shop, thin documentation

Ageless Peptides does the buying experience well: card checkout, clear bundle pricing, a free-shipping line, and fast stated handling, all in plain research-use-only language. What it does not yet do is document its products. The single lab report is real and legible but belongs to another company, and nothing visible covers the blends. We are rating it on what a buyer can verify from public pages, not on product quality, because we have not tested product. A report issued to Ageless for its own lots, covering the blends, would change this assessment.

Bottom line

Quick verdict

Reasonable for card-paying US buyers who ask for the lot report first. Hold off on the blends until a report is shown.
