Welcome to PeptideSuppliers.org: how to find a peptide supplier for your research needs

By the PeptideSuppliers.org editorial team · How we review

PeptideSuppliers.org is a searchable database of research peptide suppliers. The idea is simple: if you know what you need, you should be able to see who sells it, what documentation they publish, how they ship, and how you can pay, without opening twenty tabs.

Why we built it

Looking for a research peptide supplier usually means sorting through storefronts that all say the same things: “third-party tested,” “high purity,” “fast shipping.” Very few show you the evidence. Some publish a certificate of analysis (COA) for every lot; others show a single sample report, or nothing until you ask. Payment options range from ordinary cards to peer-to-peer apps and crypto that are hard to reverse if something goes wrong.

We wanted one place that puts those differences side by side. Right now the database covers 25 suppliers, and every one has its own review page with the details we could verify.

How to use the database

Start at the supplier database and search by supplier or compound. Then narrow the list with filters:

  • Compound: show only suppliers that list the material you are looking for, such as BPC-157, GHK-Cu, or tesamorelin.
  • COA status: separate suppliers that publish COAs from those that offer them on request or show only testing language.
  • Price tier and shipping: filter by budget, mid-range, or premium, and by whether a supplier ships outside the US.
  • Country and payment: find US or UK suppliers, and see which payment methods each accepts.
  • Ordered by us: show suppliers where we placed an order and received product.

Every card links to a full review, so you can see why a supplier looks the way it does before you click through to its site.

How we rate suppliers

Our ratings are about documentation, clarity, and buyer risk, not about product quality. We do not run our own lab tests, so we do not claim to know that any vial contains what its label says. What we can check is what a buyer can check: whether COAs exist and name a lab, whether a report is tied to a specific lot, whether policies and prices are published, and how recourse works if an order goes wrong. Our methodology page explains the approach in more detail.

Where we have ordered, we say so on the listing and write up what arrived. Where we have not, the review is based on the supplier’s public pages, and it says that too.

A short checklist for choosing a supplier

  1. Start with the exact material and form you need, then check which suppliers list it.
  2. Look for a COA that names the lab, the methods, and the lot, and ask whether the report covers the lot that will be shipped.
  3. Check what the report does and does not show. Purity, identity, and content are different measurements.
  4. Compare the delivered price, not just the sticker price: shipping, tax, bundle discounts, and minimums all change the total.
  5. Look at payment methods. Cards offer dispute options; bank transfers, crypto, and peer-to-peer apps usually do not.
  6. Read the refund and damage policy before you order, not after.

What this site is not

Everything we list is sold for laboratory research use only. We do not give dosing, administration, or medical advice, and nothing on this site is intended for human or veterinary use. See our medical disclaimer. Laws on buying and importing these materials differ by country, so check the rules where you are before ordering.
