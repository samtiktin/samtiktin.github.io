COA mass checker

Type the mass printed on a certificate of analysis. The tool shows which compounds it matches, and flags oxidation and look-alike forms. It checks identity only; it says nothing about purity, sterility or safety.

Spectra often show several charge states (for example [M+2H]2+). Use the deconvoluted or stated molecular mass where you can. Tolerance is about 1 Da, or 0.05% for large peptides.

Enter a mass to see matches.

How to use the result

A match means the stated mass is consistent with that compound. It does not prove the batch is pure, correctly folded, sterile or the right stereoisomer: for example, a peptide with a D-amino acid has the same mass as its L-form. Use the chromatogram for those questions.

An oxidation match (+16 Da per oxygen) suggests some methionine has oxidized, common in older or poorly stored batches of peptides such as MOTS-c, Semax and tesamorelin. A small amount is normal; a large share is a quality concern.

A look-alike match means the mass fits a different form than the listing, such as CJC-1295 with DAC versus without, or tesamorelin versus unmodified GHRH(1-44). Ask the supplier which form they sell.

Masses are approximate averages and vendors round differently. Read our review methodology and the compound guides for the full documentation checklist. For research use only; not medical advice.
