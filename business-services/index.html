Fulfillment and white-label

Services for brands that sell research-use-only materials.

Listing

Rapid Fulfillment

Rapid Fulfillment Inc. is a U.S. third-party logistics provider (3PL) with a California address and a second warehouse in Illinois. Its peptide program page describes white-label fulfillment for research-use-only brands: warehousing, pick and pack, labeling, lot and batch workflows, domestic shipping with temperature-controlled options when required, and ecommerce integrations. It states that it does not manufacture or sell peptides, and that products come from separate, independent supplier relationships.

Rapid Fulfillment in more detail

What the company’s page describes, section by section. These are the company’s statements, not our findings.

Operations

Storage, labeling and shipping

The page lists warehousing, pick and pack, labeling, and lot and batch workflows, with domestic shipping coordination through FedEx, UPS and USPS. It says cold-chain and expedited temperature-aware shipping are available when a product requires them. The company lists a California address and a second warehouse in St. Charles, Illinois.

Supply paths

Bring your own supply, or use a third party

Rapid says it does not require a specific supplier. A brand can send its own approved inventory, or look at what the page calls Product-on-Demand and pre-vetted bulk sourcing resources. It states these are separate supplier relationships, with their own terms and possible deposits, and that they are not required to use the fulfillment service.

Testing

Independent lab coordination

The page says independent U.S. laboratory testing and COA coordination are available, with scope depending on the product and the service chosen. It does not name the laboratories on this page. If you are a brand evaluating this, ask which laboratory, which tests, and whether reports are tied to your lot.

Integrations

Ecommerce and order flow

Rapid says it supports independent storefronts and several ecommerce platforms and APIs so orders flow into fulfillment without re-entry. Its category pages mention Shopify, WooCommerce and TikTok Shop among others. Its onboarding team is meant to confirm the right setup for a given stack.

Payments

Introductions, not a guarantee

The company says qualifying clients can be introduced to independent card-processing and B2B ACH providers, with no lock-in to a Rapid-owned processor. It adds that approval, rates and underwriting are decided by the payment provider. Treat this as an introduction, not an approval.

Costs

No setup fee, but a deposit

The page says there is no separate setup fee and no monthly fulfillment minimum, and that a $1,500 security deposit activates the account, with unused funds handled under the agreement and final reconciliation. Fulfillment, shipping, payment processing and other costs are separate. Ask for the written agreement before committing.

How the model works

The sequence the company describes, in plain terms.

Step by step

From brand to shipped order

  • A brand requests the catalog, pricing and onboarding terms
  • It chooses a supply path: its own inventory or a third-party arrangement
  • It prepares its own branding and labels, and is responsible for the claims on them
  • It connects a store and sets up payment processing with a provider of its choice
  • Orders flow to the warehouse, which picks, packs and ships
  • As volume grows, the brand may move from per-order supply to inventory it owns
Who it suits

Businesses, not individual buyers

This is a service for companies that sell research materials to others. It is not a place to buy peptides, and nothing on this page is relevant to someone choosing a vial for their own bench work. For that, use the supplier database.

Questions to ask any fulfillment provider

Useful for any business, and a fair test for the listing above.

Documentation

Traceability

  • How are lot numbers recorded from receiving to shipping?
  • Can a customer’s lot be matched to a report from an independent laboratory?
  • Who is the laboratory, and can its reports be verified with it directly?
Handling

Storage and shipping

  • What temperature does stored product sit at, and is it monitored?
  • What happens to a parcel delayed in transit?
  • How are damaged or lost shipments handled?
Commercial terms

Contract and exit

  • What exactly is the deposit held against, and when is it returned?
  • Who owns inventory held in the warehouse, and how can it be removed?
  • Which obligations stay with the brand, such as labeling and marketing claims?

What buyers should know about white-label

Why this matters even if you only buy peptides.

Brand is not manufacturer

The name on the vial may not be the maker

In a white-label arrangement, a brand sells product under its own name while a separate party makes it and another may ship it. That is a normal business model, but it means a website’s branding tells you little about who produced the material. Ask who the manufacturer is.

Documentation

A report must match the lot you receive

Lab reports belong to a specific lot. When product moves through several companies, check that the lot printed on the vial matches the lot on a report issued by an independent laboratory, and that you can verify it with that laboratory. Our methodology explains how we read reports.

Business responsibility

Compliance stays with the brand

Rapid’s page says each business remains responsible for its own products, labeling, marketing and regulatory obligations. Anyone considering this route should get their own legal advice. This site gives none.
